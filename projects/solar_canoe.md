
## Manufacturing & Assembly

![Manufacturing](../images/assembly.jpg)

A major part of this project was turning our designs into a functional 16-foot solar-powered canoe. Much of the manufacturing involved woodworking, mechanical assembly, and integrating electrical components into a structure that needed to be lightweight, stable, and reliable on the water.

### Woodworking & Structural Assembly

We used woodworking techniques to fabricate and assemble the structural components needed to support the propulsion and electrical systems. This involved measuring, cutting, fitting, and fastening wooden components together using nails and other mechanical fasteners.

One of the biggest challenges was getting everything to fit properly within the limited space of the canoe. Components that worked well in our initial designs did not always fit perfectly during assembly, so we had to make adjustments to dimensions, mounting locations, and component placement.

Throughout manufacturing, we focused on:

- Measuring and cutting wooden components to fit the canoe's geometry
- Assembling and securing structural supports using nails and mechanical fasteners
- Creating mounting structures for the motor, solar-powered electrical components, and propulsion system
- Adjusting component placement to maintain proper alignment and clearances
- Reinforcing connections and checking structural stability before testing

This process gave me experience with hands-on fabrication and taught me how important it is to design around actual manufacturing and assembly constraints rather than relying entirely on CAD models.

### Solar Power & Electrical Integration

![Electrical Assembly](../images/electrical.jpg)

The canoe's propulsion system was powered using solar energy, requiring us to integrate the solar panels, electrical components, wiring, and motor into a single functional system.

We had to consider where to position the electrical components to maintain accessibility, minimize interference with moving mechanical parts, and reduce the risk of water exposure.

A major focus was organizing and separating wiring from the mechanical drivetrain while ensuring that the electrical connections remained secure during operation.

Our electrical integration work included:

- Mounting and securing solar-powered electrical components
- Routing wiring through the canoe while avoiding moving mechanical components
- Integrating the electrical power system with the propulsion motor
- Organizing electrical connections for easier inspection and troubleshooting
- Considering water exposure, vibration, and movement during operation

Because the canoe operated on water, reliability was especially important. We had to make sure the electrical and mechanical systems could function together without interfering with one another.

### Failsafe Systems & Reliability

Another important part of the project was developing failsafe systems to improve safety and reliability during operation.

We considered potential failure scenarios involving the electrical system, propulsion components, and structural assembly. Our goal was to reduce the likelihood of a single issue compromising the entire canoe or creating an unsafe situation.

This involved:

- Planning ways to safely shut down the propulsion system if an issue occurred
- Securing electrical wiring and components to reduce the risk of disconnections
- Separating electrical components from moving mechanical assemblies
- Checking mounting points and structural connections before operation
- Developing procedures to identify and address potential failures during testing

Building these safeguards into the design helped us think beyond whether the canoe would simply operate and instead consider how it would behave when something went wrong.

### Final Assembly & Fitment

![Final Assembly](../images/canoe-final.jpg)

The final assembly required bringing together the wooden structures, solar-powered electrical system, motor, drivetrain, and propulsion components.

Getting everything to fit together was one of the most time-consuming parts of the project. Even small differences in component dimensions or mounting positions could create alignment issues that affected other parts of the assembly.

We worked through these challenges by repeatedly checking fitment, making adjustments, and testing the integrated systems.

This stage reinforced the importance of designing for assembly, maintaining appropriate tolerances, and considering how individual components interact within a larger mechanical system.
